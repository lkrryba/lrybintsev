The obvious question

Can’t I Just Ask ChatGPT?

Yes, and you should. It’ll tidy your grammar, suggest stronger verbs, and tell you your resume is looking pretty good. That last part is the problem: AI is built to be encouraging.

I had an IT teacher at school called Mr Wakefield. I’ve never forgotten him, because he told us the same thing over and over (and over) again: “Garbage in, garbage out.”

And that’s the problem with AI. It will only do what you tell it to do. Worse, it’s a yes-man. It will agree with whatever you say unless you specifically tell it not to, and then it will disagree just because you told it to. We’ve all had “You’re absolutely right!” x1000. You can see where this is going...

I’m not a yes-man (just ask my husband). I’ll give it to you straight, but with kindness. Gardener, not lawnmower.

I will tell you where your resume describes your last job beautifully and your next one not at all. Where a hiring manager stops reading. Which two of your six bullet points are doing any work, with the specific lines quoted, for you to work on.

When everyone’s resume has been smoothed by the same tools, sounding like an actual person is an advantage. Are you ready?

Because a gardener doesn’t force plants to grow or flowers to bloom. She provides the right conditions for those things to happen, and guides the plant to the right place. That’s what a good review does. Your experience is already there. It just needs light, space, and pointing in the right direction.
